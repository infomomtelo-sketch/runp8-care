What Title22 actually looks like

Seven screens, in the order you would meet them — from an empty account to the packet you hand an analyst. Every name below is invented.

📄 No PHI — Title22 holds no resident records, medications or MAR
1

Signing up for the first time

A licensing analyst can walk into your facility on any weekday morning, unannounced, and ask to see your records.

Title22 exists to answer one question before that happens: if they came today, what would they find?

Setup is one screen — facility type, licence number, capacity, address. The things that decide which Title 22 requirements apply to you.

No credit card. Fourteen days, and the clock starts the first time you actually open the app.

2

A facility to practise on

Before you put a single real record in, load the sample facility. Five staff, two incidents already filed, and a compliance checklist part-way through.

Four of those five staff have something out of date — a CPR card three weeks from lapsing, an overdue TB test, an expired First Aid card, a LiveScan that never cleared. The dashboard opens amber on purpose, so there is something to fix from the first minute.

It sits alongside your own facility — one you can break, empty and reload.

3

Inviting your staff

Five roles. A caregiver reports incidents and files documents. An in-house caregiver — your shift lead — also works the compliance checklist and logs their own training hours.

A supervisor covers staff files and training records. Read-only is what you hand an inspector.

Notice you are choosing the role. They do not pick it when they sign up — otherwise everybody would be an administrator.

4

What your caregiver sees

They accept, and they are inside your facility — not asked to create one of their own.

The first thing they see is their role and what comes with it, written out in full. Their menu is genuinely shorter: no billing, no staff files, no team list.

And they are covered by your subscription — nothing for them to pay.

5

The readiness score, and what moves it

The score is not a grade for effort. It counts requirements that are current against requirements that exist.

An expired TB test pulls it down and says whose. Tap it and you are on that person's record — no hunting through a filing cabinet.

6

Asking Tello

Ask what is outstanding in plain English and the answer is assembled from what is actually in your account — not a generic FAQ.

She will not quote a Title 22 requirement back at you as settled fact, and she will not comment on clinical or dosage matters at all. Anything regulatory goes to your licensing analyst.

7

The packet you hand over

One button. Ninety days of facility details, staff certifications and training, and incident reports.

Save it as a PDF, or print it and hand it across the table.

For your staff

The short version to send a caregiver before their first shift.

A

You have been invited

Your administrator invited you, so your account is already attached to the facility. Nothing to set up and nothing to pay.

A card on your dashboard tells you what you can do here. Your menu is short on purpose — you will not see billing or staff files, and that is not a mistake.

B

Incidents

A fall, an injury, a behaviour — filed the same shift, while you still remember it. The report records what happened and what was done about it; it does not hold a resident record, and no resident is named on the export.

If something you need is not there, ask your administrator. Roles are set by them, not by you.
